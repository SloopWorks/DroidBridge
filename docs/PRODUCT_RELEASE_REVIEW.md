# DroidBridge product and release review

Reviewed November 3, 2026; commercial recommendation updated after the owner's preference for a free trial followed by a paid tier. Recommendation: use a small free technical beta to complete verification, then a signed and notarized direct-download release with a time-limited full trial and one paid unlock. Keep spending on distribution trust and effort on setup/reliability. Feature breadth already exceeds what the first launch needs.

This is a product/release assessment, not release acceptance or an exhaustive security audit. It reviews the current working tree at HEAD `676fca3`, which contains substantial uncommitted companion improvements, the existing build-16 bundle, selected implementation paths, existing UI captures and executable evidence. No product code, installed app, phone or emulator was changed for this review. Fresh command records are under [verification/product-review-2026-11-03](../verification/product-review-2026-11-03/).

The subsequent [signing and upgrade plan](SIGNING_AND_UPGRADE_PLAN.md) incorporates checked SloopWorks enrollment/agent records, local signing identities and the current browser account. The existing organization membership already covers the membership budget; the outstanding prerequisite is Developer ID/notarization setup under the correct company account. The personal account currently visible in Chrome is a separate expired membership and should not be renewed for this project. Canonical company facts remain in [SloopWorks org-facts](/Users/patrick/workspace/SloopWorks/org-facts.md).

## Product direction

**Proposed headline: Your Android apps. Their own Mac windows.**

Supporting copy: “Use apps from your connected Android phone in separate, resizable Mac windows. Type with your keyboard and work alongside your Mac apps. No account or cloud relay required.” Immediately show Apple Silicon/macOS 26+, Android 13+, debugging authorization, and a connected phone requirement. Distinguish the optional companion from the authorization needed for app windows.

Start with technically comfortable Mac-and-Android owners who need an Android-only utility during desk work. Android developers are a useful secondary audience. A Pixel cohort is practical because the owner has hardware; broader Samsung/OEM support should follow observed testing. Enterprise deployments, games, banking/DRM compatibility and a general replacement for every phone-integration tool would create an expensive first support promise.

The advantage is the coherent Mac experience: app discovery, native windows, launch integration, reconnect and explicit handoff. Virtual displays themselves are an upstream capability: [scrcpy already documents launching apps in new/flexible displays](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy). Credit that foundation. [Vysor](https://www.vysor.io/) already offers desktop phone control and advertises $10/year or $40/lifetime Pro; [KDE Connect](https://kdeconnect.kde.org/) already covers sharing and notifications. These comparisons favor demonstrating Mac workflow quality over competing on a checklist or claiming invention of Android streaming.

**Commercial direction:** the owner prefers a free trial followed by a paid tier, provided it is manageable and practically enforceable. Recommend a 14-day full trial and one paid Mac unlock, with the Android companion included. A proposed initial price is US$29 once for two personal Macs and perpetual use of the purchased major version; later major upgrades may be paid. Price, duration and seat count are recommendations, not approved storefront terms. Prefer this simple model over subscriptions or maintaining separate feature-limited free and paid editions. Decide original-code licensing/ownership before publication; dependency source and redistribution obligations remain independent of the commercial choice. Do not promise uncrackable enforcement or classify the original code as open source without an explicit license decision.

## Trial and paid unlock feasibility

**Manageable: yes. Enforceable against ordinary casual misuse: yes, with limits. Tamper-proof on a customer-controlled Mac: no.** An application can be patched, local state can be erased, and a determined user can use upstream scrcpy separately. Charge for DroidBridge's integrated Mac experience; do not make expensive DRM a prerequisite for this budget.

Proposed product policy:

| State | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Not started | Download, inspect compatibility and complete connection setup without consuming the trial. Begin the trial on the first genuinely decoded app frame, rather than on installer download or a failed launch. |
| Trial | All supported DroidBridge features for 14 calendar days, no payment card required. Display the remaining time in the launcher/settings. The companion is included. |
| Paid | One-time unlock, proposed US$29 and two personal Mac activations. Include fixes for the purchased major version; state future-major-upgrade terms before purchase. Provide deactivate/transfer and purchase recovery. |
| Trial expired or license explicitly invalid | Refuse new paid sessions/actions with a clear purchase/activation path. Let existing work finish; retain controls for Stop, phone audio/screen restoration, saving already-received content, Forget/Revoke, settings and uninstall. Do not destroy windows or data as a timer expires. |

**Lowest-maintenance payment/licensing candidate: Lemon Squeezy, subject to store approval and seller eligibility.** It offers hosted checkout, merchant-of-record handling of payments/sales tax, generated license keys and client activation/validation/deactivation. Its published ecommerce pricing is **5% + US$0.50 per transaction, no monthly ecommerce fee**, with additional fees for some payments. At US$29 that base fee is US$1.95; US$27.05 remains before additional fees, tax effects, refunds and other business costs. This is not a margin forecast. See [pricing](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing), [fees](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees), [license API](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/api/license-api) and [store approval](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/activate-your-store). Seller bookkeeping/income-tax responsibilities and product support remain the owner's work.

Use the provider's public client License API for paid activation; do not embed merchant administration credentials in either app. Validate the exact store/product/variant and activation-instance identity, not just a successful HTTP response. Keep license keys/instance state in Keychain, omit them from diagnostics, and send licensing requests without phone serials, app catalogs, clipboard data or screen content. A random installation identifier is sufficient; avoid collecting hardware serial numbers. The provider API supports these client operations, but this review has not tested a configured DroidBridge product. See [activation](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/api/license-api/activate-license-key) and [validation](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/api/license-api/validate-license-key).

**Proposed initial enforcement:** persist trial start and last-observed time in Keychain, shared across main app/wrapper launch paths for the current Mac user. App replacement or ordinary app deletion should not restart it. Detect obvious clock rollback without treating normal small clock corrections as fraud. This is a local trial: deliberate Keychain deletion, a new OS user, OS reinstall or patching can defeat it. A strict one-trial-per-person/device guarantee needs online trial issuance and a recovery/identity policy; a random identifier alone cannot prevent repeat trial registrations.

For paid use, activate online once and cache validated entitlement locally. Proposed offline allowance: 30 days since successful validation, with quiet background refresh and clear advance notice if renewal is unavailable. Network failure is distinct from an explicit disabled/invalid license; preserve valid cached access inside that allowance. After it expires, request a connection before admitting new paid work. This recurring-connectivity requirement must be stated before purchase; it is not a subscription charge. An explicitly invalid license can block new admission immediately, while cleanup remains available. Offline revocation is necessarily delayed, and the cache is not tamper-proof. Revisit the allowance if offline customers value longer access more than rapid revocation.

**Implementation has several entry points.** Put one entitlement policy in Core and reuse it from Launcher admission, independent wrappers, retry/reconnect/restore, phone-originated launches/presets, new audio capture and companion paid effects. A launcher-only disabled button is insufficient. Recheck entitlement after awaited work before admitting a new effect, preserving existing cancellation/deadline/resource fences. Share licensing state without requiring companion pairing or a running phone service. Never run a checkout/validation request per frame or synchronously on MainActor. Existing copied wrappers need update/migration; historical free binaries cannot be retroactively made licensed by adding a new check to the current app.

The existing ad-hoc wrapper/Keychain update issues make stable signing a prerequisite for dependable shared license storage. Test trial persistence across updates/main/wrapper launch, first-frame trial start and failed-launch refusal, expiry/clock changes, activation limits, deactivation/recovery, copied-wrapper and phone-action admission, offline allowance/provider outage, explicit invalidation, and the always-available cleanup paths. Use local fake providers and the vendor's test mode before real checkout; then verify a packaged installed cohort. This is a bounded additional feature, not something already implemented or accepted by this review.

**Recommended cut:** managed checkout/paid activation plus a locally enforced trial first. No custom billing server is needed for that version. Add server-issued trial records or signed renewable entitlement receipts only if observed abuse justifies the additional infrastructure, signing-key management and support. Generated license keys alone are not vendor-signed offline receipts. Keep the activation/privacy terms aligned with the product's local device-content promise; “never contacts the internet” would no longer be accurate.

## What the review establishes

The project has a credible technical foundation: Core/Streaming/AppKit boundaries, actual VideoToolbox decoding into NSView, argument-array process execution, serial/package validation, asynchronous subprocesses, session-generation fences, exact resource ownership, a supervisor, portable bundled tools and extensive negative-path tests. Preserve scrcpy client/server **5.0.1** together. A rewrite or framework migration would consume the budget without addressing the release problems.

Current product code is approximately 11,832 lines across 75 Swift/C files plus 2,970 Kotlin/Java lines in the Android main source set. The 56 Swift test files and 116 support/build/verification scripts show substantial verification investment. These counts describe scope, not quality scores. Large, dense files such as CompanionBrokerService, Launcher, CompanionWindow, MainActivity and BridgeStore will need incremental decomposition as they change; a broad prelaunch refactor is unnecessary.

The inspected `dist/DroidBridge.app` is **0.2.0 build 16, arm64, macOS 26 minimum, ad-hoc signed, with no TeamIdentifier**. The current DMG is approximately **50.6 MiB**. Fresh offline checks passed the bundle's individual Mach-O/nested Player signatures and dependency closure; this does not establish Developer ID trust, notarization or downloaded-copy behavior. See [release inspection](../verification/product-review-2026-11-03/release-inspection.log) and [package checks](../verification/product-review-2026-11-03/package-checks.json).

Existing [build-16 evidence](../verification/companion-improvements/REPORT.md) includes installed emulator pairing, pinned TLS, decoded native video, explicit Mac clipboard/link actions, presets, verified file transfers and a completed real system Save/Finder flow. The final [Save/Finder run](../verification/companion-improvements/installed-system-save-final/results.json) passes. Earlier unsuccessful runs remain superseded evidence, not current failures of that flow. The [Pixel receipt](../verification/companion-improvements/pixel/installation.json) establishes companion installation/hash/launch only; physical end-to-end acceptance remains with the owner.

The older [release ledger](../verification/companion/RELEASE_CANDIDATE.json) still describes Mac build 10 and distribution-12 checkpoints. It must be reconciled with build 16 rather than treated as the complete current status. Neither historical passes nor this review certify the dirty working tree as a public release.

Apple documents [Developer ID distribution](https://developer.apple.com/developer-id/) and [notarization requirements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/resolving-common-notarization-issues). Its [code-signing technical note](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/technotes/tn2206/_index.html) explains the integrity consequences of modifying a signed bundle. The wrapper conclusion above is an inference from those rules and the current implementation; it is not a claim that a clean-Mac wrapper test was executed here.

Keep the current dependency bundle for the first constrained beta rather than simultaneously replacing audio/fallback. Later, a smaller controlled codec build could reduce size, older-OS barriers and redistribution work. Simply hiding the fallback UI does not remove FFmpeg: the separate audio client also uses that runtime. [FFmpeg's licensing guidance](https://ffmpeg.org/legal.html) distinguishes optional GPL components and corresponding source. Separate executables do not remove obligations for binaries shipped together; this review does not determine the license of the original Swift code.

## Bundle and delivery recommendation

Ship **one Mac product**. The primary download is a drag-to-Applications DMG; retain a ZIP as an alternate. Include the verified runtime, exact optional signed companion APK and license notices. Keep all SDK/Homebrew requirements in developer instructions. Avoid a privileged PKG installer for this per-user product. Explain the background helpers and offer guided removal so uninstall does not require terminal commands.

Offer the companion through the Mac app's explicit Install/Update action first, with a matching standalone APK as an alternate asset. Use one backed-up release signing identity and a separate `.dev` application ID for development builds. Do not ship test/attacker APKs or internal diagnostic collectors. Avoid making companion permission grants a prerequisite for the first app window. Explain that companion trust and ADB authorization are separate and that revoking one does not revoke the other.

Proposed release assets, using the final selected version rather than assuming 0.2.0 is ready:

- `DroidBridge-<version>-arm64.dmg` and `.zip`.
- Matching `DroidBridge-Companion-<version>.apk`, with package/versionCode/signer information.
- `SHA256SUMS`, versioned `release-manifest.json`, release notes and compatibility limits.
- Exact corresponding-source archive(s), build recipes/patches and notices, with their own checksums and a manifest binding them to the binary cohort.

Use **GitHub Releases** as the first public download origin after authorization. It matches existing hardcoded URLs and supports assets under 2 GiB each with no total-release or bandwidth cap documented on its [release limits page](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/releasing-projects-on-github/about-releases). Split large source bundles if needed. A public release repository can be separate from private development if that business model is chosen; still supply required dependency source.

Use the public repository README as the initial landing page. A documentation/demo site on GitHub Pages is optional; its [terms restrict primarily commercial transaction/SaaS sites](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits). A custom domain, backend, mailing platform and paid analytics are unnecessary for this beta. Keep internal Shipyard Deploy separate: missing development project provisioning is an internal channel issue, not a prerequisite for the proposed public download route.

After stable URLs and installation work, add a self-maintained Homebrew cask/tap for the technical audience. Homebrew's [Cask Cookbook](https://docs.brew.sh/Cask-Cookbook) supports versioned URLs, checksums and app bundles. This is a later convenience channel, not a way to bypass trust or acceptance checks; do not assume acceptance into the central cask repository.

## Product presentation and marketing

The inspected Android Home screen has a clear primary action and consistent Material styling. The Mac companion is functional but reads like an operations console: long explanatory paragraphs, overlapping refresh/setup controls and many peer-level sections. Improve the happy path and shorter user-facing labels before spending on cosmetic redesign. Keep technical error details available through disclosure/diagnostics rather than teaching broker/runtime terminology in every screen. Validate keyboard navigation, VoiceOver/TalkBack, contrast and enlarged text on the final candidate; existing large-text captures are useful evidence but not a complete accessibility audit.

Use a real **30–45 second demo**: connect an authorized phone, open two verified compatible Android apps in separate Mac windows, type/resize, then show one explicit handoff. Demonstrate Dock launch only after its wrapper trust and observed launch gates pass. Show the phone dependency and actual supported versions. Use app content the owner can publish, with synthetic data and no pairing QR, notification/private content or device secrets. QA fixtures establish behavior but should not become the hero marketing image.

Launch sequence and proposed success criteria (targets, not measured results):

1. **Recruit 5–10 technical testers personally.** Ask each for their OS/device/app combination and watch the download → setup → first usable window path. Track failure reason and time to that first window. Aim for at least 8 of the first 10 supported setups without developer intervention before widening reach.
2. **Publish a useful README/release page.** Place demo, one-sentence benefit, exact requirements, Download, three setup steps, limitations, privacy and support in that order. Explain “Apps run on your connected Android device” near the download. A short “DroidBridge and scrcpy” comparison should credit upstream and show the Mac workflow benefit.
3. **Share one evidence-backed launch post per relevant community.** Android-on-Mac, scrcpy and Mac utility communities are plausible audiences; check each community's current self-promotion rules first. Show HN is appropriate if the technical story and installation are ready. These are proposed outreach channels, not posts made by this review.
4. **Measure usefulness, then widen.** Ask whether testers return and use at least two work sessions the following week. Record voluntary feedback in one issue tracker, keep a small tested-app/device compatibility table, and answer common failures in one troubleshooting page. Download counts are acquisition signals, not active-user or retention counts. No account or background product telemetry is necessary for this first cohort.

Copy boundaries: say “separate Mac windows,” “no account required,” and “screen/input travel over the local device connection” where verified. Do not advertise universal Android compatibility, conversion to native macOS executables, DRM bypass, per-app audio isolation, guaranteed 60 fps/100 ms startup/battery savings, internet-remote access, or unobserved Spotlight/Cmd+Tab behavior. Public metadata lookup still contacts GitHub; a broad “never contacts the internet” claim would be inaccurate.

## Work order and release gate

**First tranche:** reconcile/freeze the candidate, decide support scope and original-code license, prototype the real signing/wrapper trust path, and fix manifest signing/version policy. Confirm the proposed trial/paid terms and payment-provider eligibility. These determine whether the advertised workflow can be distributed as promised.

**Second tranche:** simplify first use, add update/removal/support and shared trial/paid entitlement flows, establish reproducible release inputs and obtain final installed/hardware evidence. Keep later companion conveniences behind explicit opt-in; additional presets/widgets/automation are lower priority than a successful installation and second launch.

**Third tranche:** package one immutable cohort, review its source/notices, prepare the demo/release page and run the small beta. Broader marketing follows successful activation and repeat use, not completion of another feature list. Schedule by completed gates rather than promising a date before signing/hardware results are known.

Before declaring the supported scope production-ready, require all of: exact source/artifact identity; signed/notarized/stapled delivery verified after real download; copied installed-app execution outside build directories; actual decoded video and OS-routed input for advertised backends; correct session/helper/resource cleanup; retained-trust update and documented removal; physical owner checks; appropriate license/source delivery; and accurate public compatibility/privacy/support information. Unresolved optional-feature claims must be removed, labeled beta or closed with evidence. Public upload remains an owner-authorized action.
